## Findings

- **No proof-circularity in stabilization.** In `Experiments/AbstractLoopsPostprocessing.lean:75-87`, `finite_relation_chain_stabilizes` assumes only `Fintype α` and successor inclusion `relations n ⊆ relations (n+1)`. It derives full monotonicity (`80-84`) and invokes the finite-subset monotone-chain condition (`85-87`). It assumes neither an eventual-stability index nor a bound. Then `finite_scheduled_partition_stabilizes` (`90-99`) obtains the required inclusion by applying the one-step preservation theorem to each pair in the equality kernel. This is a valid finite-order argument independent of any loop claim.

- **Restriction and theorem strength are disclosed accurately.** The Lean signatures use `World : Type` with `[Fintype World]` and equality on state; they prove stabilization of the equality-kernel relation over all alternatives in that finite `World`. They do not prove the source's broader finite-reachable-image case when `World` is infinite. `C5/NOTES.md` calls out this gap, and the ledger residual at `claims.json:143-149` likewise excludes infinite `World` with finite reachable image. The indexed `step : Nat → State → State` is a supplied fixed schedule and may vary by step; it is not a theorem about an autonomous fixed-map physical loop. That distinction is also stated in the notes.

- **Postprocessing direction is correct and properly bounded.** `postprocess_preserves_kernel` (`19-24`) proves `trace a = trace b → c(trace a) = c(trace b)`, i.e. `K(trace) ⊆ K(c ∘ trace)`, matching P0037-P0039. It does not claim the converse or that all distinctions survive; the ledger ceiling/residual (`claims.json:33-45`) is appropriately one-way. The XOR pair fixture (`26-47`) treats a genuinely added coordinate as a new interface: C1's first coordinate alone is insufficient, the joint pair certifies XOR, and the pair-valued trace cannot be any function of the first coordinate. The proof compares two worlds with the same first coordinate and different second coordinates, so no closed map of the old coordinate can produce the pair. It is a finite illustration, not a general observation-acquisition theorem, as the ledger says.

- **One minor provenance gap: inherited P0014-P0019 route is not locally excerpted.** `claims.json:107-119` routes `kernelPairs` through P0014-P0019, but `C5/source-excerpts.json` contains P0037-P0046 only. The relevant P0014-P0019 text is available in C1's `source-excerpts.json`, and C5/NOTES.md says the source identity/binding is inherited from C1; this is therefore traceable in the repository but not directly hash-bound as a C5 `source_input`. To make this batch self-contained, either add P0014-P0019 to C5's excerpt file (then update its source-extraction hash) or include the exact C1 excerpt artifact and hash as an explicit inherited source input. This does not undermine the theorem or its narrow claim.
